# "The Ship Made of Ice" — original script v1 (2026-11-03)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~60 s, ~3.7 words/s), one steady voice, our own characters + story. English only, no captions.

Facts (verify each before voice):
- 1942: inventor Geoffrey Pyke proposes to Lord Mountbatten an aircraft carrier made of ice ("Project Habakkuk"),
  to cover the mid-Atlantic air gap where U-boats sank convoys. Steel and aluminium were scarce; ice was free.
- Pure ice cracks and melts. Pykrete (named after Pyke), developed with Max Perutz: ~14 % sawdust / wood pulp,
  ~86 % water by weight, frozen. Much stronger than ice, can be machined like wood, melts far slower (pulp insulates).
- Quebec Conference, August 1943: Mountbatten demonstrates to Allied chiefs; fires a pistol at a pure ice block
  (shatters) and at a pykrete block — the bullet ricochets (accounts say it narrowly missed someone in the room).
- Planned ship: about 2,000 ft (600 m) long, ~2 million tons, kept frozen by refrigeration pipes.
- 1943 prototype on Patricia Lake, Jasper, Alberta: ~60 x 30 ft, ~1,000 tons, cooled by a small motor;
  it took three summers to melt. Remains still lie on the lake bed (Parks Canada; divers visit).
- Cancelled late 1943: cost rising, needed lots of steel for insulation/cooling, longer-range planes and new
  Azores bases closed the air gap.
Sources: Wikipedia "Project Habakkuk", "Pykrete"; Parks Canada (Jasper, Patricia Lake); Perutz, "Enemy Alien" (1985).

## Script (~205 words, ~57 s)
In the middle of a war, Britain tried to build an aircraft carrier out of ice.
It's 1942, ships are sinking in the Atlantic, and steel is running out.
So an inventor named Geoffrey Pyke has an idea: ice is free, and the ocean is full of it.
The admirals point out that ice cracks.
So Pyke mixes it with sawdust, freezes it, and names it after himself.
Pykrete.
To prove it, Lord Mountbatten walks into a meeting of the top Allied generals, pulls out a pistol, and shoots a block of ice.
It shatters.
Then he shoots the block of pykrete.
The bullet bounces straight back across the room.
Luckily, it missed everyone.
Unluckily, the meeting was now very interested.
You see, ice breaks because one tiny crack can run straight through it.
Ever seen a mud brick with straw in it? The straw catches the crack before it spreads.
Sawdust does that to ice, and it keeps the cold in, too.
The plan was a ship two thousand feet long.
They built a test one in a Canadian lake.
Then planes got better, the war moved on, and the project was cancelled.
So the only ice warship ever built never fought anyone.
It just sat in a lake, and took three whole summers to lose.
