# "The Rats That Kept Their Lives": original script v1 (2026-11-06)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~65 s), one steady voice, English only, no captions. Our own characters and story.

## Facts (checked 2026-11-06 by web search; wikipedia fetches are blocked here, so each fact is cross-checked in 2+ reports)
- Hanoi, 1902, capital of French Indochina. Under Governor-General Paul Doumer (1897-1902) the French built a modern
  sewer network (~19 km by March 1902) under the European quarter. (Wikipedia; Atlas Obscura; IIAS "Rats and Sewers")
- The new pipes made a dark, predator-free home for rats, and a road into French houses; reports of rats coming up out
  of toilets in French homes. (IIAS; Saigoneer on Michael G. Vann; Made in China Journal interview with Vann)
- Plague (third pandemic) was spreading in Asia; in spring 1902 the colonial government sent paid Vietnamese crews into
  the sewers. Daily kills grew: >4,000/day in May, >10,000/day in June, a peak of 20,114 on one June day.
  (Wikipedia; everything-everywhere; Atlas Obscura; Vann, "The Great Hanoi Rat Hunt", OUP 2018)
- Then a public bounty: 1 cent per rat, but only the tail had to be handed in as proof (no corpses to deal with).
  (Wikipedia; Atlas Obscura; Culture Trip)
- Officials soon saw tailless rats in the streets: hunters cut tails off live rats and let them go to breed more. Rat
  farms appeared outside the city. The bounty was scrapped. (Wikipedia; Atlas Obscura; Saigoneer; everything-everywhere)
- Plague reached Hanoi anyway (outbreak from 1903). (Saigoneer; IIAS; Wikipedia)
- Vann calls it the "rat effect": a documented case of a perverse incentive (the "cobra effect" in India is unproven).
- Analogy: paying a gardener per weed pulled is a classic way to explain perverse incentives.

## Script
In nineteen-oh-two, a city paid people to kill rats. So people started farming rats.
Hanoi, French Indochina. The French had built a shiny new sewer under their side of town.
The rats loved it. Dark, warm, no predators. Some even popped up in French toilets.
And plague was spreading across Asia. So the city sent hunters down into the pipes.
By June, they were killing ten thousand rats a day. One day, over twenty thousand.
It wasn't enough. So the city made everyone a rat hunter. One cent per rat.
But nobody wanted piles of dead rats at city hall. So the rule was: just bring the tail.
Luckily, the tails poured in. Unluckily, the rats didn't go anywhere.
Officials started seeing rats running around with no tails.
Hunters were catching rats, cutting off the tail, and letting them go, to make more rats, with more tails.
Outside the city, people even raised rats on farms.
It's like paying a gardener for every weed. Soon, your garden is full of weeds.
The city scrapped the bounty. And plague reached Hanoi anyway.
Hanoi never paid for dead rats. It paid for tails. And a rat can live without one.

## Notes
- Hook = paying to kill rats creates rat farms; setting = new French sewer; escalation = toilets, plague, 10k then 20k
  a day; rule = just the tail; snap = Luckily/Unluckily; reveal = tailless rats; escalation = farms; analogy = weeds;
  twist/callback = it paid for tails, and a rat can live without one.
- Rats are drawn cartoonish and harmless (no gore): tails come off with a "snip" and a puff, rats run off happy.
- Colonial setting handled plainly: Vietnamese characters are the clever ones, officials are the fooled ones.
