# "The Song That Ends in 2640": original script v2 (2026-11-04)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~60-67 s), one steady voice, English only, no captions. Our own characters and story.

## Facts (checked 2026-11-04 by web search; wikipedia/aslsp.org fetches are blocked here, so each fact is cross-checked in 2+ reports)
- John Cage wrote ASLSP ("As Slow As Possible") for piano in 1985 and adapted it for organ (ORGAN²/ASLSP) in 1987. He gave
  no tempo; a typical performance lasts about an hour. Cage died in 1992. (Wikipedia "As Slow as Possible"; Classic FM)
- The Halberstadt performance, St. Burchardi church, began on 5 Sept 2001 (Cage's 89th birthday) and is planned to end
  on 4 Sept 2640: 639 years. The length honours the 639 years between Halberstadt cathedral's great organ of 1361 and the
  year 2000. (Wikipedia; universes.art; AP via Crux 2026-08; Classic FM)
- It began with a rest: the first pipes sounded on 5 Feb 2003, ~17 months after the start. (Wikipedia; Classic FM; universes.art)
- St. Burchardi is a Romanesque church from c. 1050; secularised 1808, sold 1810 and used for ~190 years as a barn,
  a storehouse, a distillery and a pigsty. (evangelisch.de 2026-08-05; familie-von-alvensleben.de; de.wikivoyage)
- A small organ was built for the project; an electric blower feeds the pipes; the keys are held down by weights/sand;
  chords change by adding or removing pipes. (Wikipedia via search; spacegroup.no; universes.art)
- One chord was held 2,527 days, until 5 Sept 2020. (Wikipedia; Classic FM 2020)
- Chord changes draw crowds (150+ packed the church in 2024 per Artforum; "thousands of visitors" per Euronews).
- 5 Aug 2026: 17th change, an A4 pipe added, from seven notes to eight; ~4 % of the piece done. Next change: 5 Oct 2027.
  (AP via Crux/WOKV 2026-08; Euronews 2026-08-05; evangelisch.de 2026-08-05; universes.art)
- Analogy: an organ pipe sounds like blowing across a bottle; while air keeps flowing, the note doesn't stop.

## Script
In a small German church, an organ is playing a song that won't finish until the year twenty-six forty.
John Cage wrote a piece called As Slow As Possible, and never said how slow.
Usually, it takes about an hour.
Then the town of Halberstadt took it literally.
Their first great organ was built in thirteen sixty-one, six hundred and thirty-nine years before two thousand.
So that's how long the song would last.
The venue, a thousand-year-old church that had spent years as a barn, and a pigsty.
They built a tiny organ, switched on an electric blower, and held the keys down with weights.
Like blowing across a bottle, as long as the air flows, the note never stops.
One chord lasted two thousand, five hundred and twenty-seven days.
And every change packs the church.
In twenty twenty-six, a crowd squeezed in to hear it go from seven notes to eight.
After twenty-five years, it's about four percent done.
Luckily, you can still hear it. Unluckily, you'll miss the ending by about six hundred years.
And here's the twist: the piece starts with a rest.
So in two thousand one, the organ began by playing absolutely nothing, for seventeen months.

## Notes
- Hook = the end date; setting = Cage's untimed score and the town's literal answer; escalation = 639 years, barn/pigsty
  church, weights on the keys, 2,527-day chord, crowds for one note; analogy = bottle; deadpan double line = Luckily/Unluckily;
  twist = the opening rest (17 months of nothing).
