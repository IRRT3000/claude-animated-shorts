## Description (YouTube / Facebook)
In Mexico City, a man fell out of the sky, straight through a family's roof... and the family beat him up.

His name was Joaquín de la Cantolla. By day he tapped out telegrams; by night he built balloons. In 1863 he rode into a bullring dressed as a charro, climbed into a balloon he had made himself, and went up. He kept flying for fifty years, through skylights, rooftops and, in 1914, rebel gunfire.

Today, thousands of little paper hot-air balloons still float up over Mexico every year. People still call them by his name.

## Fact notes (for replies)
- The 1863 ascent from the Plaza de Toros del Paseo Nuevo, under Maximilian; he arrived as a charro on horseback (that he flew WITH the horse is a legend).
- The skylight fall on Salto del Agua street and the beating are from chronicles; no date survives.
- 1914: last flight with aviator Alberto Braniff; the wind took them toward Chalco and accounts say Zapatista troops fired on the balloon. He died days later.
- "Globos de Cantolla / Cantoya" = the paper hot-air lanterns of Mexican fiestas (Academia Mexicana de la Lengua).
