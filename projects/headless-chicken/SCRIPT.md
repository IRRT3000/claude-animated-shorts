# "The Chicken That Lived Without a Head": original script v1 (2026-11-06)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~65 s), one steady voice, English only, no captions. Our own characters and story.

## Facts (checked 2026-11-06 by web search; wikipedia/snopes/britannica fetches are blocked here, so each fact is
## cross-checked in 2+ search reports)
- 10 September 1945, Fruita, Colorado: farmer Lloyd Olsen beheaded a young Wyandotte rooster, Mike (~5.5 months old),
  for supper. (Wikipedia; Snopes; WorldAtlas; Guinness World Records 2024)
- Family story (great-grandson Troy Waters to the BBC, 2015; repeated by Snopes/Guinness): Lloyd's mother-in-law was
  visiting and loved the neck, so he cut high to leave a long neck.
- The axe missed the jugular vein; most of the brain stem and one ear were left; a blood clot stopped the bleeding.
  (Wikipedia; Britannica; Discover Wildlife; Scientific American blog)
- Mike kept walking, tried to peck and preen, and "crowed" with a gurgle in his throat; he tried to tuck his head
  under his wing to sleep. (Wikipedia; Snopes; Mental Floss)
- Olsen fed him with an eyedropper: a milk-and-water mix, grain and small worms dropped straight into the gullet; mucus
  was cleared from his throat with a syringe. (Wikipedia; Snopes citing Fort Worth Star-Telegram 1945; LIFE; Britannica)
- Scientists at the University of Utah, Salt Lake City, examined him. (Wikipedia; Mental Floss; Amusing Planet)
- A sideshow promoter, Hope Wade, took Mike on tour across the US: 25 cents a look. LIFE magazine photographed him.
  (Wikipedia; LIFE.com; Snopes; Guinness)
- He grew from about 2.5 lb to almost 8 lb. (Scientific American blog; Amusing Planet; Baidu/Wikipedia)
- Copycat farmers beheaded their own chickens hoping for another Mike; none lasted. (Wikipedia; BBC via Snopes)
- March 1947, in a motel in Phoenix, Arizona, Mike choked in the night; the Olsens could not find the syringe, which
  had been left at the sideshow. He had lived ~18 months headless. (Britannica; Wikipedia; Guinness)
- Fruita still holds a yearly "Mike the Headless Chicken Festival". (Wikipedia; Guinness; RoadsideAmerica)
- NOT used: the "$4,500 a month" and "$10,000 insurance" figures (single, unverified sources).

## Script
In nineteen forty-five, a farmer went out to get dinner. Dinner did not cooperate.
Fruita, Colorado. Lloyd Olsen picked a young rooster named Mike, and raised the axe.
His mother-in-law loved the neck. So Lloyd aimed high. A little too high.
The axe took the head. But it missed the main vein, one ear, and most of the brain stem.
Mike shook it off, and went back to pecking for corn.
That night, Mike tried to sleep with his head tucked under his wing.
So Lloyd fed him with an eyedropper. Milk and water, straight down the throat.
Scientists in Salt Lake City examined him. A blood clot had saved his life. The brain stem did the rest.
Then a promoter showed up. Mike went on tour. Twenty-five cents a look.
Life magazine took his picture. Other farmers tried to make their own Mike. None of them made it.
And Mike kept growing. From two and a half pounds to almost eight. With no head.
Then, one night in a motel in Phoenix, Mike started choking.
The syringe they used to clear his throat? Left behind at the show.
Mike survived an axe for eighteen months. He didn't survive a forgotten syringe.
His town still throws him a party every year. Lloyd only wanted dinner.

## Notes
- Hook = dinner did not cooperate; setup = mother-in-law loves the neck so he aims high (real family story); reveal =
  missed vein, ear, brain stem; gag = goes back to pecking, tries to tuck a head that isn't there; care = eyedropper;
  science = clot + brain stem; escalation = tour, LIFE, copycats fail, grows to ~8 lb; turn = motel choking; twist =
  survived an axe, not a forgotten syringe; callback = he only wanted dinner.
- Tone: cartoonish, no gore. The cut happens behind a fence post with a puff of feathers; Mike's neck ends in a clean,
  rounded toon stump (pale, with a tiny bandage tuft). The choking beat is soft (coughs, a dim motel lamp), no suffering
  shown; we cut to an empty perch with one feather.
